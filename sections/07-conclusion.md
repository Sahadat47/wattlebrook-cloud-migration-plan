# 14 Conclusion

This report has presented a project plan for moving six of Wattlebrook Bank's legacy systems to AWS in its Australian regions within twelve months and AUD 4.5 million. The charter establishes measurable goals, a clear scope, success criteria, and a clear sponsor, team and stakeholders with clear roles. The plan follows the literature's recommended phased approach in three waves of migration: low-risk internal systems first, customer-facing systems last, and the approach to migrate each system (repurchase, rehost, replatform, refactor or retire) is aligned to the value and condition of the system.

The WBS segments the scope into nine work packages and 60 activities, and the CPM schedule translates these into a six milestone sequence from Charter Approval on 19 February 2027 to Closure on 28 January 2028 lasting 250 days. The critical path purposefully passes through all three waves, since the lessons learned from each wave should be implemented before the next wave begins. The schedule receives float on the largest build activities and three spare cutover weekends prior to the December change freeze, and a final deadline that allows for a cutover in the month of January. By ensuring all activities and deliverables have a clear owner and one single point of accountability, resource leveling and the RASCI matrix help ensure that the project management team does not lack support.

Sixteen risks were identified by the risk management process. The most severe risks are those with low probability and severe impact of loss of data and security breaches and regulatory non-compliance which are addressed through avoidance and robust mitigation measures, while the highest-scoring risk is un-documented dependencies between the legacy systems. An EMV analysis of the top cost risks justifies the AUD 400,000 contingency reserve, and a three-point estimate shows that the float on the internet banking refactor covers its likely overrun. Process for issue management, quality metrics and go/no-go criteria ensure that the problems remain within view and prevent any wave going live before it is ready. Lastly, the change request process, configuration management via GitHub, and the communication plan and team's handling of Jira give the regulated bank the control and transparency it requires. These factors contribute to the project having a realistic and well-controlled trajectory to retiring the bank's aging data centre before hardware support for it expires.
